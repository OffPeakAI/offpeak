THE RESEARCH CASE

Why build on spare GPUs?

Paying for computing power and putting it to useful work are different things. OFFPEAK starts with a practical question: can spare, authorized capacity run a suitable open model at a lower total cost?

OFFPEAK · PROPOSED PILOT

How that shapes OFFPEAK.

Useful output. Reliable requests. A sustainable cost. The pilot has to prove all three together.

Use capacity that is genuinely available. The plan requires permission to serve third-party work and an available request slot. A low GPU activity reading alone is not enough. Product plan ↗

Price the service, including the work that fails. Proposed prices respond to occupied serving slots, with a minimum that covers the measured cost of compute, operations, and failed work. Lower activity does not guarantee a discount. Product plan ↗

Measure before making a promise. Quality, successful requests, availability, and total cost must pass the pilot's checks. Showing a quote and a spending cap does not establish a price advantage.

Read research notes 7 primary sources

These are results from the cited research systems. OFFPEAK has not reported equivalent production benchmarks.

  1. [1] USENIX ATC · 2019

    Philly: allocated GPUs and useful work ↗

    Table 3, §3.2 reports 52.32% mean utilization of allocated GPUs in a two-month Microsoft DNN-training trace, rounded to 52.3% above. The activity metric is an upper bound on effective streaming-multiprocessor utilization. This historical study does not measure today's global GPU market, available inference slots, or authorized OFFPEAK supply.

  2. [2] ASPLOS · 2024

    SpotServe: cost and latency under interruptions ↗

    Figure 7 and §6.2 compare GPT-20B per-token costs and latency against on-demand-only serving. The authors report up to 54% lower cost, with latency increases of less than 18% on average and 90% at the 99th percentile. The evaluation replays extracted availability traces on AWS g4dn.12xlarge instances. These results depend on that workload, pricing, scheduling, and recovery design; they are not OFFPEAK prices or performance.

  3. [3] OSDI · 2024

    ServerlessLLM: making the model ready ↗

    Figure 6, §§7.1–7.2 report 3.6–8.2× faster checkpoint loading against tested PyTorch and Safetensors baselines. The microbenchmarks used FP16 OPT, LLaMA-2, and Falcon checkpoints, cold caches, RAID0 NVMe storage, and an eight-A5000 loading testbed. This measures loading weights, not token generation. The separate cluster workload was derived from an Azure serverless trace, not observed production LLM traffic.

  4. [4] MLSys · 2020

    Salus: sharing requires scheduling and memory control ↗

    Salus combines fast job switching and GPU memory sharing in a TensorFlow execution service. Its workload-specific experiments support the need to manage sharing deliberately. Its gains are not a forecast for modern language-model fleets, and this is not a claim that OFFPEAK has implemented Salus or training/inference co-tenancy.

  5. [5] OSDI · 2020

    Clockwork: predictable service needs admission control ↗

    Clockwork schedules DNN inference against latency objectives using predictable execution profiles. The useful lesson is to admit only the work a system can serve reliably. Traditional DNN execution differs from variable-length language-model generation, so its results do not establish OFFPEAK response times or reliability.

  6. [6] NVIDIA · DOCUMENTATION

    What a GPU-utilization reading actually means ↗

    NVIDIA defines GPU utilization as the portion of a sample interval during which at least one kernel executes. Our interpretation: that activity percentage alone cannot establish free model memory, an available serving slot, permission to serve, or profitable capacity. The proposed OFFPEAK load measure therefore uses occupied serving slots, with GPU activity as additional telemetry.

  7. [7] AWS · DOCUMENTATION

    Spare cloud capacity can be reclaimed ↗

    AWS can interrupt Spot Instances when it needs that capacity back; availability varies. AWS recommends fault-tolerant applications. The implication for this research case is to budget for interruption and recovery. These are AWS product semantics, not evidence that OFFPEAK uses AWS or inherits its behavior.

What the OFFPEAK plan still needs to prove

The supplied v1.0 design proposes explicitly authorized supply, a validated model revision, fresh capacity information, and a real slot reserved at request acceptance. A quote alone reserves neither funds nor capacity. Its load measure is the 60-second smoothed fraction of occupied serving slots; GPU activity is supplemental. A measured cost floor includes supplier charges, serving and payment overhead, failed work, and margin. Failed pilot requests without a completed, retrievable result are unbilled to the buyer, but their costs still belong in the provider's economics. Expansion depends on measured quality, reliable request handling, and positive contribution on real paid work. Read the proposed pilot and its boundaries ↗
